# Baseline vs. ANN Model Comparison (Phase 5)

Generated by `ml/models/train.py` against `ml/artifacts/*/metrics.json` (real computed numbers, not assumed — `docs/ML_SPEC.md` §2.7 discipline, applied here per §7/§12). All four models were fit on `data/processed/splits/train.csv` (14,729 rows) and compared on `data/processed/splits/val.csv` (3,157 rows), which none of them were fit on. The test split was touched exactly once, after this comparison and the resulting model/threshold choice were already fixed (`docs/ML_SPEC.md` §6).

## Selection rule and result

`ML_SPEC` §12: *"Recall and PR-AUC are weighted most heavily in model selection... a missed churner is more costly than an unnecessary retention touchpoint."* `ml/models/train.py::select_winner` ranks candidates by `(validation PR-AUC, validation recall)` — it does not assume the ANN wins, and it did not win here.

**Random Forest is the objective winner**: highest validation PR-AUC (0.5855, edging out the ANN's 0.5847 by 0.0008) **and** by far the highest recall (0.8158 vs. the ANN's 0.6951 — Random Forest catches ~12 more percentage points of actual churners at its threshold). Gradient Boosting has the single best ROC-AUC (0.7704) and F1 (0.5882) but does not lead on the two primary metrics.

**Honest finding on the ANN specifically:** the ANN performs competitively — it is not the worst model on any metric, and beats Logistic Regression on PR-AUC and accuracy — but it does not beat Random Forest on either of the two metrics ML_SPEC §12 weights most heavily. All four models are in fact extremely close (PR-AUC spans a narrow 0.5782-0.5855 band, a ~1.3% relative spread), which is itself a finding: on this combined IBM + synthetic-Pakistan + synthetic-India dataset, there appears to be a ceiling to how separable churn is from these 19 raw features, that no amount of extra model capacity (the ANN) or non-linearity (the tree ensembles) pushes meaningfully past what a regularized linear model already gets close to.

## A trade-off the metrics table doesn't show: artifact size

| Model | Serialized artifact size |
|---|---|
| Logistic Regression | 4 KB |
| Random Forest | **50 MB** |
| Gradient Boosting | 160 KB |
| ANN | 104 KB |

Random Forest's `n_estimators=300` forest is ~300-500x larger on disk than every other candidate, for a PR-AUC advantage over the next-best (ANN) of 0.0008 — noise-level at this sample size. `ML_SPEC` §7 flags exactly this kind of constraint for Gradient Boosting ("included only if it fits within PythonAnywhere free-tier constraints... a Phase 4 deliverable, not assumed up front"); the same reasoning applies to whichever model is actually promoted to serve traffic. This phase's selection rule is validation PR-AUC/recall only (per the objective criterion above), so Random Forest is recorded as the Phase 5 winner and saved as such — but a deployment-constrained future phase (backend/hosting, `docs/DEPLOYMENT.md`) should revisit whether Gradient Boosting or the ANN (both within 0.002-0.0016 PR-AUC of Random Forest, at 1/300th the size) are the better production trade-off once hosting constraints are actually in scope. Not resolved here — out of Phase 5 scope, recorded so it isn't silently lost.
